Method

We screen US policy texts for regression discontinuity (RD) and difference-in-differences (DiD) research, re-estimate published papers with independently written code, and study patterns across policies.

Earlier public draft (June 2026). Current figures come from the revised draft dated September 30, 2026.

Screening

AI screens policy texts for numerical thresholds, eligibility cutoffs and variation across states or time. The candidate list uses earlier assessments than the benchmark.

  1. Enacted: federal legislation needs a public-law number.
  2. No adoption wave: exclude a state policy if sampled texts show its policy type enacted in three or more states or DC within a year before or after.
  3. Outside the COVID period (2020–2021).
  4. Data availability: a named dataset must match a public source, or restricted data must be named alongside a dataset that matches no known source.
  5. Research potential: identification, policy significance, treatment intensity and data feasibility each score at least 3 of 5.

Research design

Assessments consider the research question, changes in policy exposure, potential comparison groups and available outcome data.

Checking identification

The checks need data beyond the policy text: for RD, manipulation around the cutoff and other policies at the threshold; for DiD, parallel trends, anticipation, spillovers and reasons for adoption.

Estimates and adoption

The estimate comparison uses independently written AI code and the code’s first estimate of each paper’s selected coefficient. The adoption study’s sample and analysis plan were set in advance.

Data sources

483,414 unique policy texts: 41,496 state legislative, 315,727 federal regulatory, 125,919 federal legislative and 272 state executive orders. Sources: Open States, Federal Register, Regulations.gov, eCFR and GovInfo.

State bills, 2016–2026: about 10% of enacted, non-ceremonial bills in most states; coverage varies.
